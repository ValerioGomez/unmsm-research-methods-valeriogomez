## 1. Belmont Principles Application to Botanical AI

Although the Belmont Report (1979) was originally designed for biomedical and behavioral research involving human subjects, its core principles can be translated to ecological and ethnobotanical machine learning research:

### Respect for Persons (Autonomy & Consent)
* **Translation to Ethnobotany:** The dataset contains traditional etnobotanical knowledge (plant names, traditional medicinal uses) collected from indigenous and local communities in the Peruvian Amazon. Respect for persons dictates that we honor the autonomy of these communities. We do not claim intellectual property rights over their ancestral knowledge.
* **Consent:** The dataset was collected under the institutional authority of the IIAP, which acts as a public custodian. This study uses the data strictly for non-commercial, academic research, respecting the public nature of the records.

### Beneficence (Do No Harm & Maximize Benefits)
* **Maximizing Benefits:** The automated classification of plant growth habits assists in cataloging botanical diversity and preserving ecological registries. The trained model is shared as an open-source tool to support forest conservation.
* **Minimizing Harm:** The primary risk is the exploitation of traditional knowledge for commercial pharmaceutical development (biopiracy). To mitigate this, we do not expose raw coordinates or private community names in the modeling features, and the dataset distribution is restricted to academic purposes.

### Justice (Fair Distribution of Benefits & Burdens)
* **Translation to the Global South:** Historically, botanical data from the Amazon has been exported to the Global North without benefit-sharing. By developing and hosting this reproducibility pipeline within a Peruvian academic institution (UNMSM) and designing it to assist a local research institute (IIAP), we ensure the benefits of AI research remain within the region of data origin.

## 2. Nagoya Protocol Compliance Statement

This study is designed to comply with the **Nagoya Protocol on Access to Genetic Resources and the Fair and Equitable Sharing of Benefits Arising from their Utilization**:
1. **Access Authorization:** We utilize historical, public administrative data compiled by IIAP. We do not perform new genetic or biological field sampling.
2. **Benefit Sharing:** All source code, pre-trained models, and reproducibility artifacts are shared openly with IIAP and the Peruvian scientific community.
3. **No Patenting:** The author pledges not to apply for patents or commercial intellectual property claims based on the classifications or etnobotanical correlations identified by this machine learning model.
