## 3. Key Observations & Critical Feedback

### Major Comment 3: Explicit Governance & Nagoya Protocol Alignment
* **Observation:** The protocol processes etnobotanical text descriptions (`USOS`) compiled across 18 years from indigenous and local communities in Loreto, Ucayali, and Madre de Dios.
* **Critique:** While Section 4 of Protocol v1.0 mentions open-source code sharing, open science in traditional knowledge must be carefully balanced with protection against biopiracy and unauthorized commercial exploitation.
* **Recommendation:**
  1. Add an explicit **Nagoya Protocol Compliance Statement** to Protocol v2.0, affirming that the etnobotanical data is used strictly for non-commercial academic research and cataloging.
  2. Clarify that while source code and pre-trained model weights are released under an open-source license (MIT), raw dataset redistribution remains governed by IIAP institutional custody.

## 4. Final Recommendation

**Decision:** **Accept with Minor Revisions**

Protocol v1.0 is of high scientific and ethical quality. Incorporating the explicit Nagoya Protocol governance statement will finalize the research protocol to publishable standards.
